AI feedback tone

AI feedback tone

Every analysis comes with AI-written coaching feedback. The feedback tone setting lets you choose the voice that feedback is written in, so it sounds like your coaching — supportive, direct, technical, or entirely your own.

Each coach sets their own tone. Feedback for an athlete is written in the voice of that athlete’s coach — both the first-pass feedback and any refinements you ask for. It changes only how the feedback reads — never the analysis itself, the numbers, or what appears on public reports.

Choosing a tone

Open Settings from the sidebar and find the Feedback tone section. Pick one of the presets:

  • Balanced — calm, precise, and coach-like. This is the default, and it’s the voice your feedback already uses if you’ve never changed the setting.
  • Supportive & encouraging — leads with what went well and frames misses as opportunities, while staying honest about the data.
  • Direct & concise — short sentences, straight to the point: what worked, what didn’t, what to do next.
  • Technical & analytical — backs every observation with numbers and explains the reasoning behind each recommendation.

When you select a preset, the exact instruction text that will be sent to the AI is shown below the selector — what you see is exactly what’s used. Save, and the new tone applies to feedback generated from then on.

Writing your own — “My tone”

If none of the presets sound like you, choose My tone and write your own instruction in the text box. A few tips:

  • Describe the voice, not the content: “Warm but no fluff. Keep it under four sentences per section. Always end with one concrete next step.”
  • Keep it short — up to 500 characters.
  • Plain language works best. You don’t need prompt-engineering tricks.

Your text is saved to your own settings, so you can come back and edit it any time. If you switch to a preset later, your custom text is kept and will be there when you switch back.

The tone setting works alongside custom prompts. A custom prompt shapes what the feedback covers; the tone sets the voice it’s written in. If you use a Replace default custom prompt, your replacement text fully controls the instructions — including voice — so the tone setting won’t change it.
