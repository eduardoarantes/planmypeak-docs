Reviewing a workout

Reviewing a workout

Who can use this — all coaches, for the athletes they can see. Assistant coaches review their assigned athletes; head coaches review anyone in the company.

When an activity syncs from TrainingPeaks, PlanMyPeak compares the completed session against the planned workout and produces a compliance analysis. Open an activity from your dashboard to review it.

Compliance analysis only runs once a workout has been completed and its data has synced. Open a workout that’s still planned for a future date and, in place of the compliance score, you’ll see a note that the workout hasn’t been completed yet — the score appears automatically after the athlete finishes it. You can still preview what an upcoming workout asks for before it’s ridden — see Previewing an upcoming workout. The same applies to AI feedback: it can only be generated for completed workouts. If a completed workout still shows no score, it’s usually because there was nothing to measure against (no structured plan or no power recorded). Rides without a structured plan still get a useful review — see Rides without a planned structure.

Reading the analysis

The analysis brings together everything you need to judge the session:

  • Planned vs. actual — duration, distance, training load, and intensity side by side.
  • Compliance score — an automatic 0–100 score with a color band and a plain-language verdict (for example, on target, underdone, or overdone).
  • Effort detail — depending on the data recorded, you may see a power profile, aerobic decoupling, heart-rate recovery, peak power, and a segment-by-segment Segment Compliance chart breaking each interval down against its targets.
  • Time in zone — whenever a ride has power data and the athlete has power zones, a card shows how the ride’s time was distributed across those zones. Percentages are exact; the per-zone times are approximate.
  • Matching rides — earlier sessions with the same structure, so you can see how this one compares.

Aerobic decoupling only appears for steady aerobic sessions. It measures how the power-to-heart-rate ratio drifts over a sustained Zone 2 effort, so it needs a continuous Zone 2 block of at least about 30 minutes to be meaningful. When the planned workout is an interval, threshold, or other session without a long enough Zone 2 stretch, the card shows “Aerobic decoupling not relevant in this session” instead of a number.

The activity profile chart

The Activity Profile chart plots the session against its plan. The coloured bars are the planned training zones for each interval; the lines are what the athlete actually did.

  • The zone legend below the chart names each zone using the athlete’s own power-zone setup as it was on the day of the activity — so the labels (and how many zones there are) match how that athlete trains. If an activity predates this, or has no power zones, the legend falls back to the standard names (Recovery, Endurance, Tempo, Threshold, VO2 Max, Anaerobic).
  • Use the Power, Heart Rate, Cadence, and Elevation buttons to show or hide each line. A button only appears when that data was recorded.
  • The chart always starts aligned: idle time at the start or end of the recording — pedals not turning before the ride begins, or the file left running after the cool-down — is cropped away, and the first pedal stroke lines up with the start of the planned workout. The trace is only shifted, never stretched, so it keeps its true shape and length: a ride that was genuinely shorter than the plan still ends before the right edge (a cut-short session stays visible as one), and a ride that ran long extends past the planned bars.
  • Each recorded point is drawn at the moment it actually happened, and recording pauses (a coffee stop, an auto-paused ride) show as gaps in the lines rather than a line drawn straight across — hovering over a pause shows no made-up values, and the rest of the ride is never squeezed out of position.
  • When the workout prescribes a cadence target, the chart shades a band over each interval showing the planned cadence range, with the actual cadence line on top — so you can see at a glance whether the athlete held the target (for example, staying inside an 88–95 rpm band) or drifted at peak power.
  • When the ride recorded elevation, an elevation line traces the terrain, so you can see whether a fade or a surge lined up with a climb rather than the flat. Indoor and trainer rides usually have no elevation, so the line and its button simply don’t appear.
  • Hover anywhere on the chart to read the exact power (W), heart rate (bpm), cadence (rpm), and elevation (m) at that point in the ride.

If a session has no cadence recorded and no cadence target, the chart looks exactly as before — no cadence line, band, or button. The same is true of elevation: with none recorded, there’s no elevation line or button.

Zooming in on a section of the ride

To study one stretch of the ride — an interval block, a climb, a surge — click and drag across the Activity Profile chart. The selected section is highlighted and a selection stats panel opens below the chart with numbers for just that slice:

  • Headline: duration, distance, and TSS of the selection.
  • Detail: work (kJ), normalized power (NP), intensity factor (IF), variability index (VI), efficiency factor (EF), elevation gain and loss, average grade, and climb rate (VAM).
  • MIN / AVG / MAX table: power, heart rate, cadence, speed, pace, elevation, and temperature — each row appears only when the ride recorded that data.

A few things to know:

  • Everything is computed from the ride’s own recorded samples, on the spot — selecting never changes the stored activity or its analysis.
  • NP, IF, TSS, VI, and EF need at least a minute of power inside the selection; shorter slices show a dash for those instead of a misleading number. IF and TSS also need an FTP on the activity.
  • To clear the selection, click the chart once without dragging, press Esc, or use the Clear selection button on the panel.

The Segment Compliance chart

Further down the review, a completed, analyzed workout gets a dedicated Segment Compliance chart — it replaces the old segment-by-segment list, and shows how well each interval was executed at a glance:

  • The chart redraws the planned intervals as coloured zone bars (the same layout as the Activity Profile chart above, with the same zone legend underneath naming each zone), and draws a compliance marker over each one — a line spanning exactly the interval’s width, at the segment’s compliance height on the Compliance % axis on the right (0–100). High markers mean the interval was executed as planned; low markers flag the intervals worth a closer look. The planned zone colours stay fully visible.
  • Select any interval — click or tap anywhere on its column, use the keyboard, or step with the ‹ › arrows on the details panel (handy when an interval is too narrow to click) — and the segment details panel below the chart shows everything about it: the segment name and type, how well it matched (excellent to poor, or skipped), its compliance score, the planned target versus what the athlete actually did (average power and duration), the duration compliance, and a short interpretation explaining what drove the score.
  • The first interval is selected when the page opens, so the panel is never empty — just click another interval to compare.
  • When the workout repeats an interval (the same planned power and duration more than once — say, 5×5min at threshold), a Repeated intervals table appears below, comparing the reps side by side: each rep’s average power, intensity factor (IF — normalized power relative to the athlete’s FTP, shown for efforts of a minute or longer), and average heart rate, grouped per target (warm-up and cool-down are left out — they’re book-ends, not reps). The groups fold into an accordion with one group open at a time; select a group’s header to switch. It’s the quickest way to spot a fade or HR drift across reps — and selecting a row highlights that rep on the chart, exactly like clicking the chart itself.

The Segment Compliance chart only appears once a workout has been completed and analyzed — a planned preview or an unstructured ride doesn’t show it.

Where the data comes from

At the very bottom of the review, a small footnote credits the source of the data, next to each provider’s icon. Most of the time the plan and the recorded streams come from the same place, so you’ll see a single line — for example Data from TrainingPeaks.

When the workout was planned in one provider but the power, heart-rate, and cadence streams were matched from another — typically a TrainingPeaks plan filled in with detail from a matched Strava ride — the footnote splits into two lines so it’s clear where each part came from:

  • Plan from TrainingPeaks
  • Stream data from Strava

Previewing an upcoming workout

You don’t have to wait for an athlete to ride a session to see what it asks for. Open a structured workout that’s still planned for a future date and the review page previews the plan:

  • The workout description — the coach’s notes for the session (for example, “3×10min @ Sweet Spot, keep cadence above 90”) — is shown near the top, with its line breaks preserved. The description also appears on completed workouts whenever one is present.
  • The Activity Profile chart draws the planned zone bars and the FTP line from the planned structure, exactly as they’ll appear once the workout is completed — so the preview and the post-completion analysis line up. There are no actual power, heart-rate, or cadence lines yet, because nothing has been ridden.
  • A Planned Workout summary shows the planned duration, TSS, intensity factor, and energy. Anything the plan doesn’t specify shows as a dash.
  • In place of the compliance score you’ll still see the note that the workout hasn’t been completed yet, and the Coach Feedback card explains that feedback becomes available once the workout is done — feedback is written from the completed ride, so there’s nothing to generate yet.

The planned chart needs a time-based structure (intervals measured in minutes or seconds), a power target, and the athlete’s FTP. The FTP is taken from the athlete’s most recent completed cycling ride, so an upcoming workout charts as soon as the athlete has at least one synced ride with power zones — a brand-new athlete with no completed rides yet sees the description and planned summary until their first ride syncs. When a workout is built around distance or has no power targets, the page still shows the description and the planned summary, just without the chart.

Rides without a planned structure

Not every ride follows a structured plan — free rides, group rides, and races often sync with no planned intervals at all. These activities still get a review page:

  • The Activity Profile chart plots the recorded power, heart rate, cadence, and elevation lines (with the athlete’s FTP line when their power zones are set up). There are simply no planned zone bars, because there was no plan.
  • A Ride Summary card shows the headline numbers: duration, average and max power, normalized power, intensity factor, TSS, energy, and average heart rate. Anything the device didn’t record shows as a dash.
  • The Time in Zone card breaks the ride down across the athlete’s power zones, just like on structured rides.
  • In place of the compliance score, a note explains that compliance analysis isn’t applicable — there’s no plan to score against.
  • You still get Coach Feedback: for a completed cycling ride with recorded power and an FTP, the AI writes a performance review of the ride itself — intensity, effort distribution, durability (aerobic decoupling), and peak efforts — without a compliance grade. Generate it the same way you would on a planned ride, and it publishes to TrainingPeaks as the coach summary plus the link to the athlete’s report (no compliance line).
  • You can edit and refine this performance feedback exactly like on a planned ride — tweak the wording yourself, revert to the original, or ask the AI to refine it with a note (see Refine the AI feedback). The refinement works from the same ride metrics the original review used.

AI feedback needs power and an FTP, not a plan. A completed cycling ride with a recorded power stream and an FTP gets an AI performance review even with no planned structure. When a ride has no power data or no FTP, the Coach Feedback card explains that instead of offering a Generate button.

Compliance analysis is for cycling. For other workout types — strength, swims, runs, and so on — the review page shows a Workout Summary with every metric the device recorded (duration, distance, training load, heart rate, calories, and more), without compliance or AI feedback sections, since the power-based analysis doesn’t apply to them. These workouts also never appear in your dashboard’s review queue: there is nothing to review.

Choosing between two AI versions

Every so often, a workout’s Coach Feedback card shows two versions of the feedback side by side, labelled Version A and Version B. We do this on a small, regular sample of reviews to keep improving the quality of the AI feedback — and we ask you, the coach, which one is better.

Read both, then select Choose Version A or Choose Version B to pick the one you’d rather send. A few things to know:

  • Your choice is final. Once you pick a version, it becomes the workout’s AI feedback and the other version is gone — there’s no way back to it.
  • Pick first, then refine. While both versions are showing, the Edit, Request AI Refinement, and Approve and publish actions are paused. Choose a version and they come back, working exactly as usual on the version you kept.
  • It’s blind on purpose. The two versions aren’t labelled with anything but A and B, so your choice reflects which feedback reads better — nothing else.

You’ll only see this on a handful of reviews, not every workout. The rest of the time there’s a single AI feedback draft, just as before.

Refine the AI feedback

Every analysis comes with AI-written coaching feedback — a short narrative you can send as-is or make your own. You can:

  • Edit the feedback to add your voice or a personal note.
  • Request AI Refinement — give the AI a short note (for example, “focus more on the climbs” or “make it more encouraging”) and it rewrites the feedback for you. This works on every report, whether or not the ride had a planned structure — an unstructured ride’s review is refined from its own metrics.
  • Revert to the original AI version if you want to start over.

The analysis shows which coach last edited the draft, so a team stays in sync. Head coaches can change the AI’s tone and emphasis for everyone — see Custom AI feedback.

The feedback focuses on the main work. Warm-up and cool-down are only called out when they were meaningfully under-executed — if they were done acceptably, the AI leaves them out so the feedback stays focused on the intervals. One exception: when a session has hard intervals and the warm-up beforehand was short (under about 10 minutes), the feedback adds a gentle reminder that a slightly longer warm-up next time — within reason — helps prepare for hard efforts.

Occasionally the AI feedback can’t be generated for a workout. When that happens, the Coach Feedback card shows a short notice instead of a draft, with a Retry button — the rest of the analysis (compliance score, segments, and charts) is still there. Select Retry to generate the feedback again. While feedback is unavailable, the workout can’t be approved and published yet — retry until it succeeds, then approve.

Workouts that were already in an athlete’s history when you connected them get the compliance analysis automatically, but Coach Feedback is not generated for them up front — only recent workouts get feedback automatically. For an older, imported workout the Coach Feedback card explains this and shows a Generate AI feedback button. Select it to create the feedback on demand, then review and approve as usual.

Approve and publish

When you’re happy with the feedback, select Approve and publish. A short popup shows you the exact comment that will be posted on the athlete’s TrainingPeaks activity — the compliance headline and coaching note. You can edit that text before it goes out, so the comment reads exactly how you want for this one activity. Select Confirm & publish to send it, or Cancel to go back.

The link to the athlete’s full report is always added to the end of the comment (the popup shows it and notes this), so your athlete can always open the full analysis — you don’t need to add it yourself, and it can’t be removed by editing.

The status pill at the top of the page tracks where the report is — analyzing, review pending, approved, published, or dismissed — and once it’s published you’ll see the timestamp and a link to the athlete’s report. You’ll always get a clear success or error message; if publishing fails (for example, because the connection expired), the message tells you the next step.

The report link is the same for everyone — one link opens the report for whoever follows it. The athlete opens it to read their full report; if you (the coach) open the very same link from the TrainingPeaks comment, it opens your coaching view of that activity. Either way you sign in first — the report isn’t public, so it stays private to the people allowed to see it, each seeing their own view. The full analysis lives in PlanMyPeak.

See how the athlete responded

After a report is published, your athlete can react to it from their side: a thumbs up or thumbs down on the report, plus short comments about how the session felt. When they do, an Athlete Feedback panel appears on your view of the same activity, under the coach feedback — their rating and comments, newest last.

The panel is read-only for coaches and only shows up once the athlete has actually rated or commented, so it never adds noise to reports without a response.

Dismiss a report

If you don’t want a report sent to an athlete, select Dismiss — on the activity page next to Approve and publish, or straight from the Dismiss button in your dashboard review queue. After a quick confirmation, the report is set aside: nothing is posted to TrainingPeaks, the athlete is not notified, and the activity leaves both dashboard views.

Dismissing isn’t final. Open the activity again and select Reopen to bring the report back as a draft, so you can revise it and approve it later.

Review mode vs. auto-approve

Whether a workout waits for you or is approved automatically depends on the athlete’s feedback mode. In review mode, every analysis lands in your pending queue; in auto-approve mode, well-scored sessions publish on their own. You set this per athlete — see Managing athletes.

An analysis is produced for activities that sync within about 48 hours of being completed. If an activity is much older when it arrives, it may show no report.
