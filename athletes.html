Managing athletes

Managing athletes

Who can use this — all coaches. Assistant coaches see and manage only the athletes assigned to them; head coaches see and manage every athlete in the company.

The Athletes page lists the people you coach. Athletes are imported automatically when you connect TrainingPeaks. Each athlete shows their name and email, the groups they belong to, their TrainingPeaks plan, and their status. Select an athlete to open their detail page.

Filtering your roster

All filtering lives in one rail down the left of the page, under a single rule stated at the top:

Each number is how many athletes you’d see with that option applied — with all your other filters still on. Options that would show no one are dimmed and can’t be picked, so you can never filter your way into an empty list.

You start on a sensible default — your own athletes, Active only — so you don’t open onto a list dominated by deactivated accounts. Those defaults appear as removable chips above the list, so the starting point is always visible.

The filters are:

  • Roster (head coaches only) — All coaches (everyone in your roster) or a specific coach: you, plus each assistant coach, each labelled with their role. Switching coach clears any group selection, since groups belong to one coach (see below).
  • Status — Any status, Active, or Deactivated.
  • Groups — your athlete groups. Pick one or more to narrow to their members. This section only appears when a specific coach is selected and that coach owns groups — a group’s members aren’t comparable across coaches, so it’s hidden at All coaches scope. Assistant coaches don’t have the Roster filter, so they don’t see this section.
  • TrainingPeaks plan — Premium and/or Basic, an athlete’s TrainingPeaks account level. (Premium accounts carry the detailed data the analysis uses.)
  • Also include → Former athletes — off by default. These widen your list rather than narrow it, so their count reads as a green +N. Turn it on to also see athletes who have left your roster; they appear marked Former and are read-only (they can’t be opened).
  • Search — the box above the table matches name or email.

The tally and the chips

There’s exactly one count on the page — the header tally, “N athletes shown / of M in the roster” — and the table footer restates it as “N of M athletes match X filters”. No competing numbers anywhere.

Every active filter shows as a chip in the Filtering by bar: coach, status, each group, each plan, the former-athletes toggle, and your search term. Click a chip’s × to remove just that one filter. Reset to default returns you to your own Active athletes.

If nothing matches

Because dimmed options can’t be selected, the rail alone can never empty the list. The only way to reach No athletes match these filters is the free-text search — and when that happens, the page says so and offers one-click buttons to loosen the specific filters that are on: Clear the search, Any plan, Any group, Any status, and All coaches.

Sorting

Sort the list by Name (A–Z) (the default), Name (Z–A), or Most to review — athletes with reports waiting for you first. Sorting never changes any count.

Groups

Use groups to organize your athletes — the same concept as athlete groups in TrainingPeaks. Each group belongs to one coach; when you pick a coach in the Roster filter, the Groups section lists that coach’s groups. Selecting All coaches hides the Groups section, because groups from different coaches aren’t comparable at that scope.

Importing groups from TrainingPeaks

Your TrainingPeaks athlete groups can be brought in with the PlanMyPeak Importer  browser extension. Imported groups show up with the same members — no extra setup needed.

My Athletes

Every coach has a My Athletes group that always contains your whole roster. New athletes are added to it automatically as they’re imported, so it stays complete on its own. My Athletes can’t be deleted.

Group membership

Group membership mirrors TrainingPeaks, and an athlete belongs to one group at a time — moving them into a group takes them out of the one they were in. Both the groups and who belongs to them are managed in TrainingPeaks and brought in with the importer extension; there’s no “new group” button and no add/assign control in the app.

TrainingPeaks plan

Your athletes’ TrainingPeaks account level — Premium or Basic — is a filter in its own right (under TrainingPeaks plan in the rail) and shows as a pill on each athlete’s row. It reflects TrainingPeaks automatically, so there’s nothing to set up.

The athlete page at a glance

Open an athlete and the header tells you where they stand: a pending review count that links straight to their queue, who they’re coached by, their TrainingPeaks account tier, their timezone, and a connection badge:

  • TP connected — their workouts are syncing.
  • TP reconnect — their TrainingPeaks access expired; they need to re-authorize from their own portal.
  • TP not connected — they haven’t linked TrainingPeaks yet, so nothing is syncing for them. See Connecting TrainingPeaks.

Below that, the page runs: Activation, This week, Weekly summary, Feedback approval, Training block and Training plan type, then their Activities.

Athlete status

Athletes are Active or Deactivated, set under Activation. Deactivating an athlete stops their analysis and feedback without deleting their history; you can reactivate them at any time. You can also set an Auto-deactivate date so an athlete is paused automatically — useful at the end of a training block or season — then Save date. Deactivation notifies their coach.

An athlete can only be activated once they’re linked to TrainingPeaks. If they aren’t linked yet, the Activate button is disabled and the page tells you why — connect them first (via your roster import or their own registration), then activate.

When you activate an athlete, their recent history (roughly the last 45 days) syncs in, and every completed ride gets a compliance analysis you can read on its review page. Historical workouts get the analysis only — no AI feedback is written and nothing is posted to TrainingPeaks for them. If you want AI feedback on one of those rides, open it and generate feedback from there.

Inviting an athlete by email

Athletes can have their own PlanMyPeak account, the athlete portal, where they see your approved feedback and can connect their own TrainingPeaks or Strava. You invite them from the Account invite row on the athlete’s detail page, using Send invite email (or Re-send invite email if they’ve been invited before).

Activating an athlete never emails anyone on its own. Every invite goes through a review step first, so you always see the message before it leaves.

Reviewing the invite before it goes

Send invite email opens a dialog with the exact email that will be sent: the address it’s going to, the subject line, and the message body, already filled in with the athlete’s name and yours.

You can edit the subject and the message. Our branding and the signup button are added around whatever you write, so the athlete always gets a working link. Please keep the line about signing up with the same email address the invite was sent to: that instruction is what links their account to their training data automatically. There’s no code to copy and nothing to configure.

Two things are not editable, on purpose: the signup link itself, and the address the invite goes to (always the athlete’s stored email).

Sending it yourself instead

Some athletes are far more likely to open a message that comes from your own address. The dialog supports that: use Copy message and Copy email address to take the text to your own mail client, then choose I’ll send it myself. Nothing is sent by us, and the Account invite row records that a coach has handled the invite, with the date, so nobody on your team invites them twice.

You can still send a normal invite from PlanMyPeak afterwards if you change your mind.

When an invite isn’t available

The Account invite row explains itself when there’s nothing to send: if the athlete has no email on file from TrainingPeaks, or they already created their account, it says so instead of offering the button.

If a send fails, the dialog tells you why and stays open with your text intact, so you can fix it and try again. Rapid repeat sends are blocked for a minute to protect the athlete’s inbox.

You’re also notified in the bell menu when new athletes connect to you. Athletes who register themselves are announced one at a time. Athletes brought in by a TrainingPeaks roster sync are rolled up into a single summary per sync (which also counts any new assistant coaches), so a large import never floods your notifications — and a routine re-sync only notifies you when it actually adds someone new.

How workouts get reviewed

Feedback approval on the athlete’s page decides what happens to new analyses:

  • Coach review — reports start in draft and wait in your report queue for you to approve before anything is published to TrainingPeaks.
  • Auto-approve — reports skip coach review and publish directly. Best for athletes whose compliance is stable.

Changing this applies to new reports only; reports already created keep the mode they had.

The activities list

The athlete detail page lists their synced workouts, newest first. You can filter the list by sport, and page through the full history.

By default the list shows workouts up to and including today, so you’re looking at what’s actually happened. To also see what’s coming, tick Show planned workouts — the list then includes future, planned sessions (dated tomorrow onward) alongside the completed ones. Untick it to focus back on today and earlier.

Your sport filter, the planned toggle, and the page you’re on are remembered in the page address, so you can link to or refresh a filtered view — and when you open a workout and come back, you land right where you left off.

Training block and plan type

  • Training block → Current training type records what the athlete’s current work is built around: Base Training, Sweet Spot, Tempo, Threshold, VO2 Max, Race, or Recovery — or None. It’s there so you can see at a glance what phase an athlete is in.
  • Training plan type → Current plan type marks whether the athlete is on a Custom plan or an Off-the-shelf plan, which decides which compliance alert rule applies.

Any coach assigned to an athlete can change these, as can a head coach. An athlete whose plan type is Not set is never checked for compliance alerts — so if an athlete never seems to trigger one, check this first.

Athlete progress

The athlete page also shows the bigger picture.

This week

A load progression score out of 100 with a one-line read on how the athlete is taking on their training, then this week’s TSS against their 4-week average and what’s planned for next week. Until there’s enough history it says so rather than guessing.

Weekly summary

A card for one week at a time — use the arrows in its header to step back and forward through the last twelve weeks. It shows:

  • Fitness — how much training the athlete has built up over the last several weeks. It rises steadily with consistent work and fades during time off.
  • Fatigue — how much recent, short-term load they’re carrying right now.
  • Form — the balance between the two: positive means fresh and ready, negative means loaded up and working hard.
  • Total duration and Total TSS, shown as done against planned, plus distance, bike time, elevation gain, and work.

When an athlete first connects, these numbers need a few weeks of history to settle, so early weeks read Still calibrating — treat them as rough until they’ve built up.

When the athlete’s TrainingPeaks account records a daily resting heart rate, the week also shows its average resting HR. A resting heart rate that drifts up through a heavy block is an early sign they may need more recovery. If no resting-HR data is available, it simply doesn’t appear.

Below the numbers, a chart plots the weeks side by side. Use the chips above it to choose which lines to draw — TSS, Planned TSS, Duration, Distance, Elevation, Work, and Resting HR — and select a column to jump the card to that week. Only metrics the athlete actually has data for are offered.

Weekly reports on the card

Two buttons sit on the weekly summary when there’s something to read:

  • Under-training / Over-training / Weekly summary — view notes — opens that week’s compliance report: what was planned versus done, which rides were missed or extra, other activities, and the rule that fired.
  • View stimulus report — opens a deeper read on the week: a verdict of Maintain, Modify, Reduce, or Recovery required, the week’s impact and why, a recommendation for next week, a day-by-day session breakdown, and coaching guidance (strengths, what to focus on, action items). It appears only for weeks that have one.

These help you spot trends without opening each workout one by one.
