Analysis notes

Analysis notes

PlanMyPeak already knows how to read each kind of session — what to check in a threshold set, what to ignore on a recovery spin. Analysis notes let you add what it can’t know: the things that are true of your athletes.

Your notes are added to PlanMyPeak’s own criteria, never in place of them, and they only ever apply to your own athletes. Nobody else sees them, and they never reach another coach’s riders.

Notes change what the AI takes into account. If you want to change how the feedback sounds, that’s AI feedback tone. For a one-off tweak to a single report, edit the draft in Reviewing a workout.

Where to find them

Open Analysis Notes from the sidebar. Every coach has their own.

The left rail lists what you can annotate:

  • Ride environment — Indoor and Outdoor. Whatever the session was, this applies to every one of your athletes’ rides recorded that way.
  • Ride types — recovery, endurance, threshold, VO2 max and the rest. Each one applies when a session is recognised as that kind of ride.

Pick one and you’ll see its sections. Each section shows PlanMyPeak’s own text at the top, marked Read only — that’s what the AI already does — with your My addition box underneath.

Some sections have no PlanMyPeak text at all. You’ll see a line saying so, and you can still add a note there.

What makes a good note

Write the thing a stand-in coach would need to know about your squad and that the data alone wouldn’t tell them:

  • “My riders do these on the coast road, so expect wind-driven surges.”
  • “Ignore average speed — most of my athletes train on hilly terrain.”
  • “My athletes ride indoors in erg mode, so power should be very steady.”
  • “If they miss the last interval, suggest repeating the session rather than adding volume.”

Each note is up to 500 characters, and the counter under the box tells you how much room is left. That’s deliberate: a note adds context, it doesn’t restate the criteria.

Saving, clearing, and leaving

Edit as many profiles as you like, then press Save notes — everything goes at once. Profiles with unsaved edits carry a dot in the rail and on the sidebar item, and if you try to navigate away you’ll be asked whether to keep editing, discard, or save and leave.

To remove a note, empty the box (or press Clear) and save. The note is deleted, and that section goes back to PlanMyPeak’s text alone.

Saved notes reach new reviews within about 5 minutes. Rides already reviewed keep the feedback they were given; re-analysing one picks up your current notes.

Part of a larger set of instructions

Your note is inserted into a much larger set of instructions the AI already follows — rules about evidence, wording, output format, and what it must never say to an athlete. Contradicting those built-in rules can lead to unexpected behaviour: the AI receives two conflicting orders and may follow either, inconsistently.

A note is read in the spirit of the section it sits under. A note on Do not overvalue stays context-only — the AI won’t hold it against the athlete. A note on Recommended action is only used when the session wasn’t executed as prescribed. Adding a note somewhere doesn’t change what that section is for.

So keep notes additive: what to check, what usually goes wrong, what to ignore, what to suggest next. Don’t use them to change the AI’s voice, its output format, or to make it say something the built-in rules forbid.
